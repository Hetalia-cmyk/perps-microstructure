On the latency column. It reports median market-order execution — order submission to fill acknowledgement — not settlement finality, which is often three to five orders of magnitude slower. Only five venues publish a real end-to-end figure (Hyperliquid, Vest, Ostium, GMX, Nado). Several publish a matching-engine number that is not achievable in practice because a deliberate speedbump sits in front of it: GRVT quotes <2 ms matching but delays takers 25–50 ms, and QFEX markets "microsecond-level latency" against a documented 100 ms speed bump. Where a venue publishes nothing, the estimate's basis is stated in the expanded row.

The finding that cuts across the table. Six of twenty venues — Lighter, GRVT, Pacifica, Nado, QFEX and, in paid-inverse form, Hyperliquid — have abandoned pure FCFS in favour of explicit maker protection. Four impose an asymmetric taker delay with post-only orders exempt (50 ms Nado, 25–50 ms GRVT, 100 ms QFEX, ~200 ms Pacifica); Lighter goes furthest at 0 ms for Premium makers against 140–300 ms for takers, and says outright that this is market-structure policy. Hyperliquid inverts the model, selling queue priority at up to 8 bp (≈45 ms per bp) while structurally prioritising cancels over aggressors. Adverse-selection protection, not latency racing, is now the dominant design axis in onchain perps.

Caveats worth carrying. Price-time priority is undocumented at edgeX, Extended, ApeX, Pacifica, StandX, Nado, Ondo and Aster — assumed by convention, never stated. Aster's volumes were delisted then relisted by DefiLlama over wash-trading concerns DefiLlama still calls unverifiable. Antarctic publishes no protocol spec, contracts or audits. GMTrade's OI reads between $58 m and $279 m depending on the DefiLlama endpoint, and Ostium's between $46 m and $316 m. StandX turns over ~545× its TVL monthly and pays points for orders that never fill. Ostium was drained for ~$23.75 m via forged oracle prices on 15 July 2026.

Where venues describe themselves wrongly. Three of the twenty publish an architecture claim their own settlement layer contradicts, and each favours the venue. GRVT's docs say it "leverages zk-rollups" while it is a validium that keeps transaction data offchain. StandX advertises "a fully onchain order book where every transaction, from order placement to final trade, is processed directly on the blockchain," yet order entry is a JWT-authenticated REST call and no matching contract appears in its audit scope. Derive says "Ethereum rollup" while L2BEAT declines to classify it as a rollup at all — full data goes to Celestia with no verified DA bridge. edgeX is the subtlest: its EDGE Chain is an Arbitrum Orbit L3 in AnyTrust mode behind a 2-of-5 committee, so it moved from one offchain-DA committee to another and was never a rollup. When a venue's own docs and its DA layer disagree, the DA layer is the fact.

Every load-bearing claim above — the six speedbump regimes, the four disputed architecture classifications, the Ostium and GMX incidents and the Derive matching split — was re-checked against primary sources in a separate adversarial pass, which corrected five of them. Confidence is highest on documented mechanisms and lowest on Antarctic, StandX and GMTrade, where the underlying documentation is thin or self-contradictory.
